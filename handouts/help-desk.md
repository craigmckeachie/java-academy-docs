# Help Desk: Rotating Helper Spot

## Why we're doing this

Your feedback last week asked for two things that can pull against each other:
- More chances to get help when you're stuck.
- Protected time to work at your own pace.

One of you suggested a breakout room where classmates can get help from a peer. The Help Desk is that idea, set up so it's fair to everyone, including the people doing the helping.

## How it works

- **One volunteer helper per day.** On workshop Fridays there may be two.
- **The helper is in the Help Desk breakout room for the first 15–20 minutes of each breakout.** A timer marks the end.
- **When the timer goes off, the helper goes back to their own work.** If you still need help, come to Craig.
- **Craig is always available.** The Help Desk adds another option; it doesn't replace asking the instructor.

## What the Help Desk is for (and what it isn't)

**It's for getting unstuck on your own code:** an error message you don't understand, a bug you can't find, or a step in the exercise you're not sure how to start.

**It's not a re-teach of the lesson.** If the topic we just covered didn't click, that's completely normal. Come to **Craig** and he'll go over it with you again. If he's tied up, he may ask someone to help out.

**You share your screen and you type.** The helper never takes over or types the code for you. It's easy to slip into "just tell me what to type" or into watching someone else work, and neither one helps you learn. You stay in the driver's seat; the helper is the navigator.

## Being a helper

- **It's voluntary.** Sign up for 1–2 days a week at most, or not at all. Both are completely fine.
- **Nobody is the helper every day.** On your off days, protect your own time. If someone asks you for help, it's OK to say: *"Today's helper is ___. Grab them or Craig."*
- **They share their screen and type; you guide.** Don't take over, and don't dictate code line by line. Help by asking questions:
  - "What do you think this line does?"
  - "What did you expect to happen? What actually happened?"
  - "Where in the workbook is this covered?"
  - "Want to step through it in the debugger together?"
- **It's fine not to know.** "Let's ask Craig" is a great answer.
- **Helping counts.** Explaining code is one of the best ways to understand it, and it's a skill employers look for.

## Getting help

- **Try for about 10 minutes first.** Reread the workbook section and run your code.
- **Bring a specific question,** such as "My loop never stops," and show what you've already tried.
- **Be ready to share your screen.** You'll do the typing.
- **Ask early.** Being stuck is normal, and asking is a skill.
